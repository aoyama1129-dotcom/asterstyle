PEOPLE / KYOTO · MAIZURU

Daiki Kambayashi

President, Maizuru Shokuseikatsu Co., Ltd. / Fish wholesaler

The sea's changing seasons,
brought to the table.

Born in Ine, Kyoto, with his family home in one of Ine’s traditional funaya boathouses, he went from running restaurants to bidding for fish at the port auction.

He ships fish selected at Maizuru Port across Japan. At his Sakana Terrace shops, the catch of the day can be enjoyed on the same day. Daiki Kambayashi's work is about bringing people closer to the sea and those who live from it.

01 / THE BEGINNING

Building a business
in Maizuru.

Daiki Kambayashi was born in Ine, Kyoto, where his family home is a traditional funaya boathouse. While studying at Kyoto Sangyo University, he aspired to start a business. He left university and set off on a bicycle journey around Japan. After an accident on the road, he eventually began building a business in Maizuru.

He began to see new possibilities in the local community. He started a food-service business as an individual in 2012, and incorporated it as Maizuru Shokuseikatsu Co., Ltd. in 2021. His first work was not fish wholesaling, but cooking and serving food to people.

02 / FROM AUCTION TO TABLE

Standing at the auction himself,
bringing in the day's catch.

He went to Maizuru Fish Market almost every morning to source fish for his restaurants. Days spent watching the auction and searching for the fish he wanted drew him deeper into the seafood trade. Eventually he obtained the right to bid as a wholesaler, and today his colleagues also stand in the auction hall.

The fish bought at auction are supplied to restaurants and supermarkets across the country. At his own Sakana Terrace stores, meanwhile, customers can choose fish and have it prepared to eat. The original Nishi-Maizuru shop and a location at the Red Brick Park connect his roles as wholesaler, fishmonger and restaurateur.

03 / THE SEA AND THE TABLE

Behind the fish,
the sea and the work of people.

Even in the same waters around Maizuru, the fish that arrive change with the seasons and from day to day. Knowing where a fish lived, who caught it and how it was handled at the port changes how we experience its flavor.

At the SHIZEN WO SHOKUSU Maizuru gathering in Kagurazaka, Tokyo, on October 14, 2026, chefs prepared seafood from Wakasa Bay brought by Kambayashi. Around the table, guests talked about the sea and their food.

“The pleasure of a dish doesn't end on the plate. Behind it are the sea, the fishermen, the handling of the catch at port, and everyone involved in distribution.”Daiki Kambayashi / From Ichiro Erokumae's article on the SHIZEN WO SHOKUSU Maizuru gathering, October 14, 2026
04 / THE NEXT GENERATION

Opening the door to seafood work
for the next generation.

Kambayashi is concerned with more than moving fish through the supply chain. He wants his company to be a place for people who would like to work at auctions, become chefs or create new products—even if they have no previous experience, provided they love fish.

A SMOUT interview published in November 2026 describes how an employee from a different industry learned the market business and eventually came to bid at auctions. The company also plans to develop processed products using fish from Maizuru.

From learning to love fish to making a living with them. Opening the world of the port to others is part of Kambayashi's work.
