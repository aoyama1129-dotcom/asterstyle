Keisuke Seno

Chef / Due ligne+

Chef of Due ligne+ in Tokyo, Keisuke Seno works across French and Italian cooking, drawing on Japanese and Chinese techniques. At the Aso Akaushi gathering, his thoughtful cooking moved the very people who raised the beef.

Crossing traditions, staying close to the ingredient.

Keisuke Seno runs Due ligne+ in Ushigome-Yanagicho, Tokyo. Trained in French cuisine, he honed his craft at Michelin-starred restaurants in Alsace and Burgundy before going on to study Italian cooking. He has also explored Chinese and Japanese techniques, bringing influences from different culinary traditions into a style of his own.

Seno opened Due ligne in Yotsuya-sanchome in 2011 and relocated to Ushigome-Yanagicho in 2021. Today he runs his intimate restaurant largely on his own. Beneath the freedom of his cooking lies a consistent question: how best to respond to the ingredient in front of him.

At the Aso table, the cooking reached the producer.

For the SHIZEN WO SHOKUSU Aso Akaushi gathering, Seno prepared a consommé from beef trimmings and aromatic vegetables, pairing it with straw-seared bonito and celeriac. Through the metallic nuances of bonito and the earthy scent of celeriac, he sought to evoke the “blood of Akaushi” — a reflection on the life behind the food.

He also served Akaushi beef cheek braised in red wine without added butter. The dish balanced depth with lightness. Producer Ryuhei Yoshida, who raised the beef, said it was the most moving braised dish he had ever tasted.

The ingredient he had worked so hard to raise took on a new expression in the hands of a chef. That response from the producer captured what the gathering set out to do: bring the people behind the food together around one table.
